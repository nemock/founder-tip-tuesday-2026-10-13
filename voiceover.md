# Founder Tip Tuesday: week of October 13, 2026

The spoken source is `script.json`; this is the readable transcript.

## Chapter 1: Open

It's Founder Tip Tuesday. Vercel handed its inbound sales leads to an AI agent, and the team answering them went from ten people to one and a quarter. The agent's first instructions came from Vercel's best rep, who wrote the rules down.

## Chapter 2: The build

Vercel built it with one engineer, spending about a fifth of his time on it. Jeanne DeWitt Grosser, the company's chief operating officer, walked Tomasz Tunguz through the build on his Office Hours show. Those first instructions ran around a hundred and twenty-five lines, written by the best SDR on the team. An SDR is the rep who qualifies a new lead before a salesperson ever sees it.

## Chapter 3: The comparison

Then Vercel trained the agent the way you'd train a new rep. In Grosser's telling, a manager reads every one of a new hire's first hundred emails while teaching the job. Once the hire is competent, the manager reads a random one in a hundred.

## Chapter 4: Train it like a new rep

For the first phase, the agent researched each lead, qualified it and drafted the reply, and the top reps reviewed its work before anything went out. The work began in June. Six weeks of that gave the team enough data and enough fixes to drop to reading one in a hundred, and by August the agent was sending on its own.

## Chapter 5: A year later

Over the next year, Vercel added products and moved up into enterprise customers, and the agent's instructions grew to a thousand lines. Grosser says the agent stopped following some of the rules buried in all that text. Many of those rules never needed an AI. They had the same answer every time.

## Chapter 6: The split

So Vercel pulled those rules out of the instructions and wrote them as code. If a lead comes from a company that already has an open deal, the system checks Salesforce and sends the lead straight to that account's salesperson. The agent doesn't get a vote. It only handles the calls that used to need a person's judgment.

## Chapter 7: Today

Inbound now runs on fourteen of those rules. Because the rules are explicit, a second agent can watch for breaks, and when one gets broken it either fixes the problem or signs off on the exception. And the reps who came off inbound were all promoted to outbound selling, skipping the year they'd normally wait.

## Chapter 8: This week

This week, pick one decision your team makes over and over, like whether a quote request is worth answering. Ask the person who makes it best to write down how they decide. Then go down that list and mark which lines are rules, with the same answer every time, and which need judgment. The rules go into a checklist or into code. The judgment is the only part you hand to a new hire or to software.
