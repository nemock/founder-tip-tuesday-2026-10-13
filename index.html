    Founder Tip Tuesday

    Vercel's best rep wrote the rules first.

    Vercel handed its inbound sales leads to an AI agent, and the team answering them went from ten people to 1.25. The agent's first instructions came from the best rep on the team.

    Jeanne DeWitt Grosser, Vercel COO

    One engineer. The best rep's rules.

    One engineer spent about a fifth of his time on the build. The first instructions ran roughly 125 lines, written by the team's top SDR, the rep who qualifies a new lead before a salesperson sees it.

    Office Hours with Tomasz Tunguz, October 2026
    "I probably read 100% of that person's first hundred emails"
    Jeanne DeWitt Grosser, COO, Vercel

    Her comparison for training the agent: a manager reads a new rep's first hundred emails, then a random one in a hundred once the rep is competent.

    June to August

    Train it like a new rep

    1. 1
      The agent researches, qualifies and drafts
    2. 2

    3. 3
      After six weeks, read one in a hundred
    A year later
    1,000
    lines of instructions, and the agent skipped some rules. Many of those rules had the same answer every time.
    The split

    Rules go in code. Judgment stays with the agent.

    Rule in the instructions

    The agent may skip it.

    Rule in code

    It runs every time. A lead from an account with an open deal goes straight to that account's rep.

    Today

    Fourteen rules, and a second agent watching them.

    When a rule gets broken, the second agent either fixes the problem or signs off on the exception. The reps who came off inbound were promoted to outbound selling.

    This week

    Write the rules down

    • Pick one decision your team repeats
    • Your best person writes how they decide
    • Mark each line: rule or judgment
    • Rules go into a checklist or code
    From my seat
